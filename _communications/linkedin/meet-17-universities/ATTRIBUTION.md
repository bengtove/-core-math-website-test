## Licence assessment and outstanding issue

The recorded copyright licences allow this collection and the described cropping subject to the notices above. ShareAlike is addressed per component; it is not an unresolved mismatch among the photographs. This does not constitute unconditional clearance for uploading to LinkedIn.

The existing register explicitly says LinkedIn platform-term compatibility is not confirmed for the 16 copyrighted photographs. [LinkedIn User Agreement §3.1](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) requests transferable and sublicensable rights. [Creative Commons guidance](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#do-i-need-to-worry-about-website-terms-of-service-when-i-share-cc-licensed-content-on-social-media-platforms) explains that CC licences do not allow sublicensing of third-party copyright, while also noting that sharing third-party CC content on social media is often possible. This is a contract-scope uncertainty, not a finding that CC photographs are universally forbidden on LinkedIn. No separate resolution was found in the archived records. The visual collage is approved and the communication files are finalised, but unconditional LinkedIn upload clearance remains unconfirmed. Confirmed obligations are attribution, preservation of licence/copyright notices, identification of modifications and the applicable per-cell ShareAlike/copyleft terms. The unresolved point is the scope of the platform contract, not a missing photo licence or a finding that all CC content is prohibited on LinkedIn. No additional permissions have been requested.

No model/property releases are recorded. Keep the intended factual university-network context; do not imply endorsement by people shown. The Zambia image includes identifiable people at source resolution.
