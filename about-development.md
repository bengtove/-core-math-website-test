# The development of CoRE-Math

CoRE-Math builds on more than three decades of collaboration in mathematics among universities in Africa and Europe. It did not emerge from a single project or programme. Its foundations include support for mathematics departments and postgraduate training, regional networks, bilateral research cooperation, scientific mobility, research collaboration and initiatives connecting mathematics with industry and society.

The Eastern Africa Universities Mathematics Programme (EAUMP), long-standing African–Nordic partnerships and activities supported by international organisations, notably the Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics (ICTP), contributed to the network from which CoRE-Math emerged.

## Early African–Nordic collaboration

Some collaborations that later became part of the CoRE-Math network date back to the early 1990s.

Makerere University and University of Bergen collaborated in mathematics through the Norwegian NUFU programme from 1992 to 2007, with activities in postgraduate training and research.

A separate Norwegian collaboration focused on mathematical modelling. The Southern African Master’s Programme in Mathematical Modelling, hosted by University of Zimbabwe in collaboration with University of Oslo, ran from 1996 to 2007 with NUFU support. It trained students from Malawi, Tanzania, Uganda and Zimbabwe; 60 students completed the Master’s programme and ten continued to PhD degrees. From 2008 to 2014, a regional Master’s programme in Mathematical Modelling based at University of Dar es Salaam followed, with technical support from University of Oslo and funding through the Norwegian NOMA programme. It trained 155 students from the region.

## EAUMP and regional collaboration

The Eastern Africa Universities Mathematics Programme (EAUMP) was established in 2002 by Makerere University, University of Dar es Salaam and University of Nairobi. With support from the International Science Programme (ISP) at Uppsala University, EAUMP connected mathematics departments that had previously received individual support from ISP.

Kigali Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) joined EAUMP in 2008, followed by University of Zambia in 2009. In 2013, KIST and National University of Rwanda (NUR), together with other institutions, merged to form University of Rwanda. Rwandan participation in EAUMP and ISP support continued through the new university.

[Regional summer schools]({{ "/education/schools/" | relative_url }}) have been organised regularly since 2004. Supported by ISP and other organisations, with ICTP involved from the beginning, they bring together postgraduate students and lecturers from different universities and provide access to advanced mathematical topics that may not be available at participants’ home institutions. EAUMP has also provided a framework for conferences, workshops and regional scientific collaboration.

Alongside EAUMP, ISP began supporting mathematics at Addis Ababa University in 2005. Although not an EAUMP member, Addis Ababa University became part of the wider collaboration. The establishment of PhD training in mathematics at the university was a major development.

## Sida-supported bilateral research collaboration

Sweden’s bilateral research cooperation with universities in Rwanda, Uganda and Tanzania contributed to the development of postgraduate mathematics education and research. These programmes primarily aimed to strengthen research capacity at participating universities. Linköping University coordinated the mathematics projects within the bilateral programmes, in collaboration with the African partner universities.

Mathematics was included in the cooperation with Rwanda from 2007, initially at National University of Rwanda (NUR) and, following the 2013 reorganisation, at University of Rwanda (UR). In Uganda, mathematics formed a smaller component of the 2010–2015 programme, followed by a more substantial collaboration in 2015–2022. Centred at Makerere University, the latter involved five Ugandan public universities in its later phase: Makerere University, Busitema University, Gulu University, Kyambogo University and Mbarara University of Science and Technology. In Tanzania, the mathematics collaboration began in 2015 at University of Dar es Salaam, formed part of the 2015–2024 bilateral programme and continued in the programme phase beginning in 2024.

The programmes supported PhD training, supervision and research collaboration, alongside postgraduate education and curriculum development. The Ugandan collaboration also supported postdoctoral research, while the Tanzanian collaboration included scientific mobility. In Rwanda, NUR and Linköping University jointly developed an MSc programme in mathematics, with teaching beginning in 2008. The programme continued after Swedish support ended in 2019. PhD training initially relied largely on the sandwich model, combining research at the home university with periods abroad. Eleven sandwich PhD students in mathematics graduated, followed by three graduates through the national PhD programme.

Beyond their institutional objectives, the bilateral programmes created opportunities for collaboration across countries. PhD programmes combining coursework and research at University of Rwanda, Makerere University and University of Dar es Salaam were developed jointly through the programmes. MSc curricula were also reviewed and developed with Swedish partners, including at a joint review in Arusha in 2016. Mathematicians from the wider network participated in the Inter-University Council for East Africa (IUCEA) benchmarking exercise in 2018–2019, which developed benchmarks for BSc, MSc and PhD programmes in mathematics.

## Developing research collaboration

Research collaboration across the wider network was strengthened through workshops in Sweden, Uganda and Ethiopia. Organised as joint ISP and bilateral-programme activities, they brought together mathematicians from EAUMP, Addis Ababa University, institutions participating in the bilateral programmes and other universities.

This process led to the establishment of [four ISP-funded international research groups]({{ "/research/" | relative_url }}) in 2019:

- **APREA** — Applied Probability Research in Eastern Africa
- **CoRS** — Combinatorics Research Studio
- **EAALG** — East African Algebra Research Group
- **PDEAPP** — Partial Differential Equations and Applications

CoRS was subsequently renamed the African Enumerative Combinatorics Community (AECC). The groups organised collaboration around mathematical research interests rather than individual institutions or funding programmes.

## Finnish collaboration and industrial mathematics

Collaboration between Eastern African universities and Finnish mathematicians, particularly at LUT University, contributed to applied mathematics and mathematical modelling in the region.

Beginning in 2007, the East Africa Technomathematics collaboration, coordinated by LUT University, initially worked with University of Dar es Salaam and later expanded to institutions in Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, Tanzania and Uganda. Activities included short visits by MSc students to Finland, curriculum development and training in applied mathematics and mathematical modelling.

The collaboration introduced Modelling Weeks, in which students worked collaboratively on open-ended problems using mathematical modelling and computational methods. The format also developed teamwork and communication skills.

The HEI-ICI project *Mathematics Education and Working Life Relevance in East Africa* developed this work further through applications-oriented mathematics education, curriculum development and links between university mathematics and working life.

The collaboration continued through *Working Life Interaction in Modelling and Data Skills* (WOLIMODS) in 2022–2024, supported by the Finnish National Agency for Education. African partners included University of Dar es Salaam, Nelson Mandela African Institution of Science and Technology and University of Rwanda; Finnish partners were LUT University, Tampere University and University of Jyväskylä. The project combined doctoral training and mobility with problem-based mathematical modelling and data skills.

The first Study Group with Industry in East Africa was held in Tanzania in 2022. Companies and other organisations presented problems for teams of mathematicians and students to investigate, creating opportunities for research, student projects and longer-term collaboration. These activities helped establish the [industrial mathematics work]({{ "/programmes/study-groups-with-industry/" | relative_url }}) that continues within CoRE-Math.

## Mathematics for Sustainable Development

The *[Mathematics for Sustainable Development]({{ "/projects/math4sdg/" | relative_url }})* (Math4SDG) project, funded by Norad through NORHED II for 2021–2026, brought together University of Bergen, Makerere University and University of Dar es Salaam. It built on long-standing Norwegian–African collaboration while broadening its focus.

The project supported PhD training in mathematics and mathematics education, outreach and activities applying mathematics to sustainable development challenges. Its closing conference, the Second African–Nordic Conference in Mathematics, was held in Arusha in August 2026.

## Mathematics competitions and outreach

Mathematics competitions extended the collaboration to school students, teachers and national mathematics communities. Work in Rwanda developed through the bilateral programme and contributed to the establishment of a national mathematics competition. The East African Mathematics Olympiad (EAMO) subsequently brought together countries in Eastern Africa around mathematical problem solving and competition.

These activities complemented the network’s work in research and postgraduate education and form part of CoRE-Math’s [outreach activities]({{ "/outreach/" | relative_url }}). Further information is available on the website’s [dedicated mathematics competitions page]({{ "/programmes/mathematics-competitions/" | relative_url }}).

## The establishment of CoRE-Math

By 2024, collaborations among mathematics departments, research groups and individual researchers had established a network spanning institutions in Africa and Europe. This provided the basis for an application to establish an ARUA–The Guild Cluster of Research Excellence in mathematics.

The application was submitted on 26 August 2024 under the name Eastern Africa Universities Mathematics Programme (EAUMP), and was approved on 3 September 2024.

CoRE-Math built on EAUMP and other existing collaborations but was not simply a renaming of EAUMP. While EAUMP had focused on Eastern Africa, the new Cluster was established to strengthen mathematical collaboration across Africa and between Africa and Europe. Its name, the Africa–Europe Cluster of Research Excellence in Mathematics (CoRE-Math), reflects this broader geographical scope.

At its establishment, CoRE-Math brought together six African universities—Makerere University, Addis Ababa University, University of Dar es Salaam, University of Nairobi, University of Rwanda and University of Zambia—and eight European universities: Uppsala University, University of Amsterdam, University of Bergen, University of Groningen, LUT University, Linköping University, University of Oslo and Stockholm University.

CoRE-Math builds on more than three decades of collaboration developed and sustained by African and European universities in research, postgraduate education, institutional development and engagement with industry and society. The establishment of the Cluster in 2024 provided a common framework for strengthening these collaborations and extending them to additional institutions and countries across Africa and Europe.
